# Demo Script

## Demo Title

Regulatory Reporting Variance Commentary: From AI Use Case Intake To Conditional Approval

## Demo Audience

AI governance, data governance, compliance, model risk, internal audit, and executive technology/risk leaders.

## Narrative

1. A finance reporting team wants to use AI to draft variance commentary.
2. The workflow draws governed data signals from platforms such as Snowflake, Databricks, AWS, or Azure: lineage, sensitive tags, data quality, report ownership, and usage context.
3. The app classifies the workflow as high risk because it touches regulated reporting, financial impact, sensitive data, and human approval.
4. The Data Governance Agent assesses whether the underlying data is fit for AI under a federated data model.
5. The app identifies gaps: incomplete lineage, CDE certification, sensitivity controls, metadata contracts, and policy-to-action ownership.
6. Risk and control mapping turns those gaps into owners, controls, evidence, cadence, and escalation triggers.
7. Model strategy recommends local extraction, mid-tier drafting, and frontier reasoning only where justified.
8. Token governance shows budget burn and spike monitoring.
9. Shadow AI tracking shows unmanaged demand and converts useful patterns into governed intake.
10. Testing and early warning signals show what passed, what failed, and what blocks monitored launch.
11. Lessons learned turn findings into training, policy updates, and better future behavior.
12. The governance memo summarizes the decision: conditional approval pending evidence closure.
13. The audit trail proves who did what, when, and why.

## Lines To Use

- “The product is not trying to be another AI chat interface. It is an operating layer for governing AI work.”
- “The question is not only whether the model works. The question is whether the workflow is governed enough to approve.”
- “This turns policy into owners, controls, evidence, cadence, and decisions.”
- “Token usage is treated like cloud spend: governed, monitored, and escalated.”
- “Federated data ownership is visible in the workflow instead of buried in committee documents.”
- “Snowflake and Databricks can tell you what data exists and how it moved. This app helps decide whether AI can use it, who must approve it, and what evidence proves control.”
- “This is the last-mile layer from governed data to governed AI action.”
- “Shadow AI is treated as a signal. Risky usage is contained, useful demand is converted into governed workflows.”
- “Every incident, failed eval, and shadow AI pattern should teach the enterprise something.”

## What To Avoid Saying

- Do not imply this is production-ready until auth, persistence, deployment, and export are complete.
- Do not overclaim regulatory compliance.
- Do not sell it as a generic AI agent platform.
- Do not imply it replaces Snowflake, Databricks, Purview, Collibra, Alation, AWS, or Azure governance capabilities.
- Do not lead with every module. Lead with the approval workflow.
